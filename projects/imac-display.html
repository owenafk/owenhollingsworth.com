iMac display

A 2017 27-inch iMac getting a second life as a 5K display. The computer inside comes out, a controller board drives the original 5K panel, and the iMac's own speakers and fan setup stay.

The goal

My monitor is a 27-inch Apple Thunderbolt Display: only 2K, with no modern inputs. Apple has used nearly the same 27-inch 5K panel since 2014, first in the 27-inch iMac through 2020 and now in the Studio Display, which costs well over $1,500 as just a monitor.

I picked up a 27-inch 5K iMac for about $200 and bought the parts to turn it into almost the same thing, for a fraction of the price.

How it works

I'm taking the computer out of the iMac and replacing it with a JRY-W9RQUHD-SA1 controller board, which drives the original 5K panel from DisplayPort, USB-C or HDMI. The iMac's own speakers and fan setup stay, with a 24 V-to-12 V converter feeding the fan controller. The new board and its connections are held by a mix of printed parts I found online and custom parts I designed.

Credit where it's due: the controller board holders are maikisch's remix, for the 2017 iMac, of StoneTaskin's original design. The crossover and step-down converter mounts and the fan vent are montezemolo's design, and the power supply supports are professorshroom's. All of them are linked below.

Key decisions

  • One cable, like a Studio Display. I designed a secure plug that fills the hole left by the iMac's Ethernet port and holds a right-angle USB4 connector wired to the board's input. One Thunderbolt cable from my MacBook to the back of the monitor carries video and full control of the monitor, and charges the MacBook over USB-C Power Delivery.
  • Menu buttons in the old USB holes. Custom buttons I designed fit where the iMac's USB ports were, with a holder for the controller's button board behind them, so I can use the board's on-screen menu without modifying the outside of the case.
  • Keep the iMac's speakers and fan setup. The display keeps the iMac's sound and cooling instead of relying on the board's own.

Problems and fixes

  • The power converter didn't fit its mount. The 24 V-to-12 V converter for the fan controller was smaller than the mounting holes on the printed part I'd downloaded for it. I got two of its corner posts screwed down, and since it's a lightweight board, two screws were enough.
  • I fried the fan by trusting the internet. The iMac's fan doesn't use a standard computer fan plug, and its wires aren't in the standard order. I wired it to the fan controller in the order someone had posted online, and it was wrong: the fan died. Since it was already dead, I cut it open to trace the real pinout: VIN, GND, O/P and PWM. I wired the replacement fan in that order, and everything worked. Lesson learned: verify a pinout yourself before trusting a forum post.
  • The buttons took a dozen tries. It took almost a dozen revisions to get buttons that fit the old USB holes and a holder that lines the controller's button board up behind them.

Where it is now

Most of the conversion is done. I'm now designing how to hold the ports from the inside before I have the RAM door cover laser cut.

What's next

  • Laser-cut ports into the RAM door cover, so the new inputs come out the back cleanly.
